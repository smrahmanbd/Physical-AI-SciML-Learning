Physics-informed neural networks

LearnsA solution that fits data and obeys an equation
NeedsGoverning equation, some data, collocation points
StrengthSparse data, inverse problems, no mesh
Best forFusing physics and data; estimating parameters
SoftwareDeepXDE, PhysicsNeMo, PyTorch, JAX

How it works (animation)

Discussion

A network ŷ(x, t) is trained on a loss with two parts: misfit to measurements and the residual of the governing equation at collocation points, computed by automatic differentiation.

Strengths: works with sparse, noisy data; solves inverse problems by making unknown parameters trainable; needs no mesh. Limits: training can be slow and fragile for sharp fronts and long times; for simple forward problems classical solvers are usually faster and more accurate.

Use PINNs when you must combine physics with data or infer what cannot be measured directly.

Equation discovery (SINDy)

LearnsA short, readable governing equation
NeedsState time series and a candidate library
StrengthInterpretable laws from data
Best forFinding unknown dynamics
SoftwarePySINDy

How it works (animation)

Discussion

SINDy assumes the law is a sparse combination of candidate terms. It fits all terms by least squares, removes those below a threshold, and refits until only a few remain.

Strengths: gives equations people can read, check and simulate; needs relatively little data. Limits: sensitive to noise because derivatives must be estimated; the library must contain the right terms.

Use it to discover or check the form of a law, for example car-following or pedestrian interactions.

Neural operators

LearnsA map from input functions to solution functions
NeedsMany input–solution pairs from a solver
StrengthInstant solutions for new inputs
Best forMany-query surrogates of PDE solvers
SoftwareDeepXDE, neuraloperator, PhysicsNeMo

How it works (animation)

Discussion

A neural operator learns G: f ↦ u, for example from a source term to the PDE solution. DeepONet multiplies a branch network (encoding f) by a trunk network (encoding the location); Fourier neural operators learn filters in frequency space.

Strengths: once trained, solve new cases in milliseconds; FNOs are resolution-independent. Limits: need many training pairs; can fail for inputs unlike the training set.

Use them when the same PDE must be solved many times for different inputs: design, control, uncertainty studies.

Gaussian processes

LearnsA smooth function with uncertainty
NeedsTens to thousands of observations, a kernel
StrengthCalibrated error bars
Best forEmulators, Bayesian calibration, design of experiments
Softwarescikit-learn, GPyTorch, GPy

How it works (animation)

Discussion

A Gaussian process is a distribution over functions defined by a kernel. Conditioning on data gives a predicted mean and variance everywhere in closed form.

Strengths: honest uncertainty that grows away from data; hyperparameters tuned by marginal likelihood; works with few data. Limits: cost grows as n³; standard kernels struggle in many dimensions.

Use GPs for emulating slow simulators such as GCAM, calibrating models and choosing the next experiment.

Hybrid models

LearnsThe part a physics model gets wrong
NeedsA sound physics model and some data
StrengthSafe extrapolation with few data
Best forGood-but-imperfect physics
SoftwareAny ML library + your physics code

How it works (animation)

Discussion

A hybrid keeps a physics model and adds a learned correction (residual learning), learned parameters, or learned closure terms inside the equations.

Strengths: needs fewer data than pure ML and behaves like the physics where data are missing. Limits: if the physics is badly wrong, the correction dominates and the benefits vanish.

Use hybrids when the physics captures the main structure and extrapolation matters.

Surrogates and emulators

LearnsA slow model's input–output map
NeedsA few runs at well-chosen inputs
StrengthThousands of fast queries
Best forOptimisation, uncertainty, policy exploration
SoftwareSMT, scikit-learn, GPyTorch

How it works (animation)

Discussion

A surrogate is trained on a few runs of an expensive simulator, using radial basis functions, Gaussian processes, polynomial chaos or networks, then queried thousands of times.

Strengths: turns months of computing into seconds; uncertainty-aware surrogates guide where to run next. Limits: valid only inside the sampled input range; accuracy depends on smoothness and design.

Use surrogates for design optimisation, sensitivity analysis and scenario exploration with slow models.

Data assimilation and filtering

LearnsThe current state of a system, continuously
NeedsA dynamic model and streaming measurements
StrengthReal-time, robust to sensor gaps
Best forPhysical AI, digital twins, state estimation
SoftwareFilterPy, DAPPER, custom code

How it works (animation)

Discussion

Data assimilation alternates prediction with a physics model and correction with measurements, weighting each by its uncertainty. The Kalman filter is optimal for linear Gaussian systems; ensemble and particle filters handle nonlinear ones.

Strengths: runs in real time, gives uncertainty, survives sensor gaps by running the model. Limits: needs a reasonable model and noise estimates; tuning matters.

Use it inside Physical AI systems and digital twins: vehicles, traffic estimation, signal control.

Quiz: who am I?

1. "I fit the data and, at many collocation points, penalise any breach of a known equation." Who am I?

It is Physics-informed neural networks.

2. "I fit all candidate terms, throw away the small ones, and hand you a short readable equation." Who am I?

It is Equation discovery (SINDy).

3. "I learn a map from whole input functions to whole solutions, with a branch net and a trunk net." Who am I?

It is Neural operators.

4. "I give you a prediction and an honest error bar that widens far from the data." Who am I?

It is Gaussian processes.

5. "I keep a physics model and learn only what it gets wrong." Who am I?

It is Hybrid models.

6. "I learn a slow simulator's input-output map from a few runs and answer in milliseconds." Who am I?

It is Surrogates and emulators.

7. "I predict with physics, then correct with each noisy measurement, weighted by trust." Who am I?

It is Data assimilation and filtering.
