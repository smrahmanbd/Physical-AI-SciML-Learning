Research brief

In this module you will

  • discuss the limits of emulators for policy analysis
Spoken overview
Read the transcript

Integrated assessment models, or IAMs, such as GCAM, the Global Change Analysis Model developed at the Pacific Northwest National Laboratory, represent how energy, agriculture and land, water, the economy and the climate interact across the world's regions until the end of the century. They answer questions such as: what carbon price would cut transport emissions by half by 2050, and what would it do to electricity demand and land use? Each scenario can take hours to run, yet policy questions need thousands of runs to explore combinations of carbon prices, technology costs and behaviour, and to quantify uncertainty. Emulators make this feasible. We run GCAM at a carefully chosen set of inputs, train a fast statistical model, often a Gaussian process, on the outputs, and use it for everything else. The emulator's uncertainty tells us where it is unsure, so we run GCAM next exactly there: this is active learning. In our toy example, five well-placed runs already give an emulator error near one percent. In this module you will decide how many runs to buy, and where.

What GCAM represents

GCAM (Calvin et al. 2019) is an open-source integrated assessment model with 32 energy-economy regions, hundreds of land and water units, and detailed technology choices in buildings, industry and transport. Markets clear every five years; emissions feed a simple climate model. Its transport sector represents passenger and freight modes and vehicle technologies, competing on cost through logit choice. Because everything is linked, a carbon price changes vehicle choices, electricity generation and land use at once.

Building the emulator

Pick an output, here transport CO₂ in 2050 versus carbon price. Run the full model at a few prices, fit a Gaussian process (module 2.6) and check it on extra runs. In the explorer: 3 evenly spread runs give an error of about 0.08 Gt; 5 runs about 0.013 Gt (around 1%); 5 runs bunched at low prices about 0.2 Gt, because the high-price region is unexplored.

Active learning

Start with three runs, then repeatedly run the full model where the emulator's uncertainty is largest. In the lab the error falls from 0.085 Gt (3 runs) to about 0.01 Gt (7 runs), with each new run placed automatically in the least-known region. With many inputs (carbon price, battery cost, biofuel availability, behaviour), space-filling designs and dimension reduction are needed first.

Limits and good practice

An emulator is only as good as the model it copies: it inherits GCAM's assumptions and cannot correct structural errors. It is valid only inside the sampled input ranges. Multi-output emulators must keep physical consistency (shares summing to one, emissions consistent with energy). Report emulator uncertainty alongside model uncertainty, and validate on held-out full runs. Published GCAM emulators and 'fast IAM' surrogates are an active research area.

Research notes

  • GCAM links energy, land, water, economy and climate across regions, but runs are slow.
  • A Gaussian process emulator trained on a few runs answers thousands of policy questions.
  • Active learning places new runs where the emulator is least certain.
  • Emulators inherit the model's assumptions and are valid only inside sampled ranges.

New words

Integrated assessment model (IAM)
A model linking energy, economy, land use and climate to evaluate policies.
GCAM
The Global Change Analysis Model, an open-source IAM from PNNL's Joint Global Change Research Institute.
Emulator
A fast statistical approximation of a slow simulation model.
Active learning
Choosing new model runs where an emulator is most uncertain.
Carbon price
A charge per tonne of CO₂ emitted, used in scenarios to drive emission reductions.
