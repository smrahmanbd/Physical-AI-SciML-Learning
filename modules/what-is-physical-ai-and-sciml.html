Spoken overview
Read the transcript

Engineers have always had two ways to predict the world. The first is physics: write down the laws, such as conservation of mass or Newton's laws, and solve them. The second is data: collect measurements and fit a pattern. Physics models extrapolate well but miss details we did not model. Data models capture details but fail where they have no data. Scientific machine learning, or SciML, combines the two. It builds machine learning models that respect physical laws, discovers equations from data, and builds fast surrogates of slow simulations. Physical AI goes one step further: it puts such models inside machines that sense and act in the physical world, such as robots, drones and automated vehicles, where mistakes have physical consequences. In transport, SciML estimates traffic from a few sensors, predicts crash risk, emulates climate and energy models, and runs digital twins of cities. In this module you will slide a model from pure data to pure physics and see where each one breaks.

Two ways to predict

Physics-based models start from laws: mass is conserved, force equals mass times acceleration, heat flows from hot to cold. They work even in situations never measured, but only describe what we put in. Data-driven models learn patterns from examples. They capture details nobody wrote down, but they can be badly wrong outside the range of their data.

Scientific machine learning

Scientific machine learning (SciML) combines the two. Its main tools, which you will meet in this course:

  • Physics-informed neural networks: networks trained to fit data and obey an equation.
  • Equation discovery: finding the law itself from data.
  • Operator learning and surrogates: fast stand-ins for slow simulators.
  • Hybrid models: physics plus a learned correction.
  • Uncertainty quantification: saying how sure we are.

Physical AI

Physical AI is artificial intelligence that perceives and acts in the physical world: robots, drones, automated vehicles, smart traffic signals. Its models must respect physics (a car cannot stop instantly), run in real time and be safe when wrong. That is why SciML and Physical AI belong together: physical structure makes learned models more reliable.

Why transport needs it

Transport is full of physics (traffic waves, vehicle dynamics, energy) and full of data (detectors, cameras, phones), but data are always sparse where it matters: during crashes, at extreme densities, in future climates. Go 3 applies SciML to traffic estimation, automated vehicles, safety, energy and emissions, integrated assessment models such as GCAM, crowds and digital twins.

Research angle

Many transport problems are 'small data' problems in disguise: plenty of normal data, very little about the rare events we care about. Physics is how we bridge that gap.

Words and links

New words

Scientific machine learning (SciML)
Machine learning methods that combine data with scientific knowledge such as physical laws.
Physical AI
AI systems that perceive and act in the physical world, such as robots and automated vehicles.
Data-driven model
A model learned from examples without built-in laws.
Physics-based model
A model built from physical laws and equations.
Extrapolation
Predicting outside the range of the data used to build a model.
